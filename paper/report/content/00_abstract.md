# Abstract

SignBridge is a research prototype that goes in both directions between Indian Sign Language (ISL) and English in a web browser. For sign to English, the webcam is read in the browser with MediaPipe Holistic, only hand and body landmarks (never video) are sent to a FastAPI server, and a one-dimensional convolutional network trained on the INCLUDE dataset (262 classes) recognizes one sign at a time, with a continuous mode that cuts a landmark stream into signs. For English to sign, a spaCy rule layer converts a sentence into ISL gloss and a 3D avatar plays one retargeted clip per gloss. Gloss is the only representation the two directions share.

On the authors' predefined split of INCLUDE (signer independence not verified) the live network reaches a top-1 accuracy of {{j:seed0_model_metrics.json:baseline_cnn.test.top1_accuracy:.4f}} and, at the confidence threshold of 0.7, answers 0.8676 of the test samples with 0.9548 accuracy. The report then measures what a deployment changes. On 100 test videos at that threshold the same model answers 0.82 correctly from stored features, 0.78 from browser landmarks of 16:9 frames, 0.65 from 4:3 frames and 0.07 from 4:3 frames when the picture aspect is ignored, with 29 percent confident wrong answers. The report documents the implementation, 367 backend and 137 frontend automated tests, the limits of the work (no face features, no signer-reviewed clips, an unresolved dataset licence) and the steps to run and deploy the system.
