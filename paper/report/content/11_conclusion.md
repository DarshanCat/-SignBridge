# Conclusion

SignBridge shows that a bidirectional ISL and English prototype can run in a web browser with the user's video staying on their own machine, with a shared gloss representation between recognition and generation. The INCLUDE dataset was processed completely and audited (4,284 samples, 262 classes), and a one-dimensional CNN trained on it reaches a top-1 accuracy of {{j:seed0_model_metrics.json:baseline_cnn.test.top1_accuracy:.4f}} on the authors' predefined split, where the BiGRU and the Transformer reach {{j:seed0_model_metrics.json:baseline_bigru.test.top1_accuracy:.4f}} and {{j:seed0_model_metrics.json:baseline_transformer.test.top1_accuracy:.4f}}. With a confidence threshold of 0.7 chosen on validation data, the live recognizer answers 0.8676 of the test samples with 0.9548 accuracy and reports the rest as unrecognized.

The most important result of the project is about measurement. A recognizer that answers 0.82 of 100 test videos correctly from stored features (0.92 top-1) answers only 0.07 of the same videos correctly (0.10 top-1), with 29 percent confident wrong answers, when it receives landmarks from a 4:3 picture whose aspect is ignored. A geometric rescale removes that failure, but a loss remains in 4:3 pictures (0.65 against 0.82 offline) that a validation test traces mostly to the black bars of the letterboxed benchmark pictures, so the loss on real 4:3 cameras is unmeasured, and the model is very sensitive to vertical scale and to signers who appear small in the picture. These findings became product behavior: a camera request at 16:9, the rescale, a framing guide inside the tested range and a size warning. A targeted augmentation was developed from the measurements and evaluated on three architectures; over three seeds the augmentation lifts the CNN on the unrescaled 4:3 picture from {{3s:cnn_base.br4x3_raw}} to {{3s:cnn_aug.br4x3_raw}} but shows no clear change elsewhere, and it does not replace the rescale.

The limits are as important as the results. No clip and no text rule has been reviewed by a signer, face features are not used, the dataset has no signer identifiers, the dataset licence is unresolved and a real webcam with real signers has not been measured. The next steps are to record signers with identifiers, to review the clips and rules with the Deaf community, and to deploy only after the problems listed in Chapter 10 have been resolved. The report, the code, the evaluation scripts and the results are arranged so that these steps can be taken without redoing the work described here.
